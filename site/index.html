What does Moon actually set?+

The calendar. Moon is one count running underneath everything, read as the year, the 13 moons and the day, locked to the real moon. Every moment on it is a Moonmark. Your day runs on the light: Light counts up from first light, Dark counts up from sunset.

What is a Moonmark?+

One exact moment on the Moon engine, the same for everyone on Earth, like 1928.05.03 09:12:04.205379905. Use it for launches, records, logs and proof of when something happened. Every Moonmark has its own link, so it fits in a QR code.

What are Light and Dark?+

How you live the day on Moon. At first light, when the sun clears the horizon, the Light counter starts at 00:00:00 and counts up until sunset. Then Dark starts at 00:00:00 and counts up until the next first light, which begins the next Moon day. A meeting at Light 06:45 always lands at the same point in the daylight. In winter there is less Light, so Light 12:00 doesn't exist. Where the sun doesn't rise or set for weeks, the day starts at solar midnight.

Why hasn't this been done before?+

It has, in pieces. Japan used daylight and night hours until 1873. Italy counted hours from sunset into the 1800s, and Babylonian hours counted from sunrise. They faded because clocks couldn't follow a moving sunrise and railroads needed one shared clock. Today every phone knows the exact sunrise where you stand, and the Moonmark gives everyone one shared moment. We didn't invent living by the light. We made it work again.

What is a Moon second?+

1 Moon second = 1.0403730490 SI seconds. It is derived, not chosen: 13 mean lunar cycles (13 × 29.530588853 days) divided into exactly 369 days of 86,400 Moon seconds. A Moon minute is 62.42 s, a Moon hour 1 h 2 min 25 s, and a Moon day 24 h 58 min 8 s in standard time.

Why 369 days?+

The moon has two natural months. The sidereal month, one orbit measured against the stars, is 27.321661 days. The synodic month, new moon to new moon, is 29.530589 days. Their harmonic mean, the correct way to average two rates, is 2ab ÷ (a + b) = 28.3832 days, and 13 × 28.3832 = 368.98 days. Moon makes that a whole 369, so each moon is 28.3846 Moon days, within 2 minutes of the astronomical value. The Moon second is then sized so those 369 days span exactly 13 real lunations.

Why is it the year 1928?+

Moon and the old calendar both start counting at year 0, so they cover the same stretch of history. The difference is size. A Moon second is a little longer than an old second, so every Moon day and every Moon year is longer too. Bigger years means fewer of them fit in the same stretch. The time the old calendar counts as 2026 years, Moon counts as 1,928 bigger ones. That's why it's Year 1928.

Is 369 connected to Nikola Tesla?+

Tesla was famously drawn to the number three. Biographers describe him circling a block three times before entering a building and preferring hotel rooms with numbers divisible by three. The widely shared quote about the "magnificence of 3, 6 and 9" has no known source in his writings or interviews, so we do not attribute it to him. Moon's 369 comes from the moon's orbit, not numerology. The echo of Tesla is a fitting coincidence.

Does Moon stay locked to the real moon?+

Yes. Each moon is the mean synodic month, the average time from new moon to new moon, and the count starts on a mean new moon. Individual new moons arrive up to about 14 hours early or late because the lunar orbit is elliptical, but that offset swings back and forth and never accumulates.

When did Moon time begin?+

Year 0, Moon 1, Day 1, 00:00:00 is the first mean new moon of 0 AD: 23 January 0000 at 02:44 UTC. It is counted back exactly 24,736 lunations from the astronomical reference new moon of 6 January 2000, 18:14 UTC. Today is Year {{y}}.

Why does my phone show a different time?+

Your phone shows the old clock, set by time zones and daylight saving. Moon doesn't use it. A Moon day is about 58 minutes longer than one turn of the Earth, so Moon never tries to tell you the hour. The light does that, and the Moonmark is the exact moment underneath.

What happens to the seasons?+

A Moon year is 369 Moon days. Each Moon day is a little longer than a turn of the Earth, so the year runs about 19 days past the sun's year. The seasons drift slowly through the moons and come back to the same moon about every 20 years. Light and Dark show your season where you stand, and every equinox and solstice gets its own Moonmark.

How do 28.38-day moons work with whole days?+

Each day belongs to the moon it starts in. That gives eight moons of 28 days and five of 29 (moons 1, 3, 6, 8 and 11), totaling 369. The pattern is identical every year.

Where did time zones go?+

Nowhere you need them. To meet someone anywhere, share a Moonmark: one moment, the same for everyone. To know if they are up, look at their light, like Tokyo at Light 00:45. Sunrise and sunset only depend on where you stand, so Light and Dark need a location, never a time zone.

How accurate is it, and how do I convert?+

Moon is defined from the SI second, which is set by cesium atomic clocks, and computed from Unix time so every system gets the same answer. Moon seconds = (Unix ms − epoch ms) ÷ 1040.3730490. The epoch is −62,165,308,562,979 ms.
