02

WHY IT CAN'T BE FAKED

A signature is math, not a promise. Here is what makes it hold.

Only Moon can sign

Signing needs Moon's private keys, which no one else has. Every receipt carries two signatures, Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65, NIST's post-quantum standard, so forging one means breaking both.

Change one bit, it breaks

The signature covers the file's fingerprint, the exact moment and the key together. Edit any of them and the check fails.

Can't be signed early

Every receipt includes the latest value from drand, a public randomness beacon run by the League of Entropy. No one can know it in advance, so a receipt provably wasn't signed before it.

Nothing hidden

The signing and checking code is open source on GitHub. Anyone can read exactly what a signature means and how it's made.

03

WHAT IT PROVES

A signed Moonmark is precise about what it says. That's what makes it strong.

IT PROVES

This exact file existed by the Moonmark, and the receipt was signed after the drand round inside it. Every receipt sits in a window a few seconds wide, bounded on both sides. Change a single pixel or character and the proof no longer matches.

IT DOES NOT PROVE

Who made the file, or that it's original. Pair a signed Moonmark with your name, your account or your contract for that.

MOON'S PROMISES

What you can rely on, for every receipt Moon has ever signed.

If Moon ever moves to a new key, the old one stays published as retired, so every receipt it signed keeps checking.

06

BUILT TO UPGRADE

Cryptography ages. Quantum computers will one day break the signatures most of the internet uses today. Moon is designed to upgrade its cryptography without breaking anything it has already signed. How Moon is quantum ready.

Two signatures today

Every receipt is signed twice over the same bytes: Ed25519, today's standard, and ML-DSA-65, NIST's post-quantum standard (FIPS 204). A receipt counts only when both check out, so forging one means breaking both.

Every receipt names its method

Each receipt starts with its version, like moonmark-receipt/3. That tells anyone exactly how to check it, so Moon can adopt new cryptography without breaking a single old receipt.

Old receipts keep working

Every version Moon has ever issued stays verifiable. Retired keys stay published, and checkers accept every past version.

Re-sealed over time

Send any receipt to POST /v1/reseal and Moon signs it again with today's algorithms and logs it on the Tideline. Re-seal again as algorithms change, and a receipt from 2026 stays provable in 2056. How.

07

THE KEY PROMISE

A signed Moonmark is only as good as the key behind it. These are Moon's commitments about its signing keys, for anyone who relies on a receipt years from now.

Every key, published forever

Every public key Moon has ever signed with stays published, here and at /v1/keys, marked active or retired. A key is never removed, so every receipt it signed can always be checked.

Retired keys never sign again

When a key is retired it stops signing for good. Receipts it signed before stay valid, and checkers keep accepting them.

Changes announced ahead

Moon announces a planned key change on this page and in the API at least 30 days before it happens, with the date the new key takes over.

If a key is ever exposed

Moon retires it at once and publishes the time it believes the key was exposed. Receipts signed before then, with their drand beacon and their place in the Tideline, remain evidence of when they were made.

Keys stay secret

Moon's private keys are kept as encrypted secrets on its servers. They never appear in its public code, its logs or its answers.

Two algorithms at once

Receipts are signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 together, so one weakened algorithm is not enough to forge a receipt. When either is retired, the other carries on while a replacement is added.

Questions about a key, or a report that one may be exposed: moon369org@proton.me.

08

STILL LOCKED TO THE MOON

Moon divides time into equal lunations, each as long as the average lunar month. The real Moon runs a little fast or slow from month to month, because its orbit is an ellipse tugged by the Sun. That wobble is expected. Moon would only be drifting if the average gap moved over the years.

Loading the Moon's new moons…

Real new moons from Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, chapter 49, checked against every solar eclipse from 1000 to 3000. The same test runs with every change to Moon, and fails if the average ever drifts. Data: /v1/drift.
